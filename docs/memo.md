**Subject:** Where the two hires should go

## Recommendation

Do not give the two hires to Billing. Put them in Logistics, and fix how the chat bot routes "I paid but it never arrived" tickets before the next hiring round. The data does not support Billing as the biggest team.

## Why

**1. Billing's queue is not all billing.** My tool read the customer's own message on all 11,641 tickets. About 39% of the tickets sent to Billing are really delivery problems, usually "money was deducted, nothing arrived". The bot sees the word "paid" and tags it Billing. A second check that does not depend on the tool's labels: 29% of the tickets routed to Billing were actually resolved by a Logistics agent.

**2. Once tickets are put in the right place, Logistics is the busiest team.** The bot's tags show Billing at 21% of tickets and Logistics at 16%. By the real problem, Billing is about 17% and Logistics is roughly 29% to 34%. Per agent, over the last six months:

| Team | Tickets per agent per month |
|---|---|
| Logistics | 42 to 50 |
| Billing | 32 |
| Returns Desk | 24 |
| Email | 14 |
| Voice | 14 |
| Chat | 11 |

Neha's view that Logistics is the team drowning is right.

**3. The misrouting has a visible cost.** A ticket sent to Billing is handed on to another team 0.41 times, against 0.11 for every other team. That is about 139 avoidable hand-offs a quarter, roughly Rs 42,000 a quarter at the Rs 305 policy cost. This is small by itself and leaves out customer waiting time and agent time lost in the wrong queue.

**4. The pressure is growing with orders, not from a quality problem.** Orders are up about 80% since mid-2025 and tickets have roughly doubled, but tickets per 100 orders stayed in the 75 to 105 range. Headcount has been 44 for the whole 18 months, so the load will not ease on its own.

## The number to aim at

Bring Billing's hand-offs down from 0.41 per ticket to 0.11, the level of every other team. That is worth about Rs 42,000 a quarter in handling cost, and it makes sure the Rs 9 lakh a year for two hires goes to the team that needs it.

## What I would do

1. **Trial for two weeks:** the tool suggests the right team when a ticket is created and the agent confirms. Track hand-offs.
2. **Both hires to Logistics.** That brings Logistics from about 50 to about 36 tickets per agent per month, and Billing stays at 32.
3. **Before hiring from outside**, test moving two Chat agents to Logistics for a month. Chat agents handle the fewest tickets. Chat runs 24x7 and has the highest first-response miss rate (13%), so check cover first.

## How sure am I

- The tool put tickets in the right category about 90% of the time on 50 fresh tickets I checked by hand (given the small sample, somewhere between 79% and 96%). The bot's own tag matched about 73%.
- The per-agent figures count tickets, not effort. Logistics tickets stay open for days waiting on couriers, and Billing tickets may take more minutes each. There is no time-per-ticket data in the export, so ask Sameer for it before signing.
- I treated order cancellations and address changes as Logistics work. If they are not, Logistics is 42 per agent rather than 50, still the highest.
- Escalations & Warranty is excluded from the comparison, as your policy says.

## What I looked at and left alone

First-response misses cost about Rs 75,000 a quarter in credits, but no single hour, channel or shift stood out enough to point to one fix. Repeat contacts, bad manufacturing lots and refund-plus-replacement cases need more time than I had.

*Note on the 22%: the export includes 139 Billing-only tickets from 2024. With them Billing is 21.8% of tickets, without them 20.8%.*
